How hops work

Your connection takes several hops to reach a server: your PC, your router, your provider's network, then the server. Every hop adds a little delay. If one hop is slow or drops data, everything after it feels it: pages load slower, calls break up and games lag. Trace shows which hop is slowing things down, so you know whether it's your Wi-Fi, your provider or something further out. This picture is an example, not a test of this PC.

What it does for you

Answers you can act on, not numbers to stare at

A stuttering call or a stalled stream needs one answer: Wi-Fi, router, or the wider internet. HonestPing checks whether the internet is answering and says what it found.

Full bars · no usable path

When the bars look fine and nothing works

Signal strength is not a working path. HonestPing separates “Wi-Fi is up” from “the internet is answering,” so you know whether to reboot or wait.

You get: a plain verdict plus the checks that back it up.

Evidence, not vibes

Status you can point at

“Your internet is responding.” Or it isn’t. Ping, jitter, and packet loss sit next to the verdict, so you (or whoever’s helping you) can see the same evidence without translating jargon first.

You get: shared clarity for family, support, or your own notes.

History that settles arguments

Was it a blip, or a pattern?

A single bad second doesn’t mean an outage. A quiet chart with a spike tells a different story than an afternoon of red. HonestPing keeps recent connection history so you can see what actually happened.

You get: context before you change cables, providers, or plans.

Something’s off

A next step when it’s not healthy

Overview doesn’t dump a wall of metrics and walk away. When things look shaky or down, you get a short explanation and a sensible next move: troubleshoot, dig into tools, or confirm it’s outside your house.

You get: a faster path to fixed.

The gap in the market

Built for the space in between

Most people are stuck between three incomplete options: the Wi-Fi icon on the taskbar, a one-time speed test that shows a single moment, or heavy monitoring built for IT desks, not for someone trying to finish a video call at home. HonestPing fills that middle: consumer-friendly, continuously honest, and explained in plain language.

What you already have

A short guide

What type of internet user are you?

See what speed a kind of home usually needs, so you can tell whether a plan fits. Most homes are a mix. Pick the one that sounds most like your busiest evening. A 7-day Pro report answers it for your home. It’s paid, and not part of Free or the first 30 days of Pro.

Light user

Browsing, email, video calls. A plan around 100 Mbps usually fits.

Streamer

4K streaming on a few screens. Evenings matter most. 100 to 300 Mbps usually fits. For streaming.

Gamer

Online games, voice chat, and game updates. Steady ping matters more than raw speed. 100 to 300 Mbps usually fits. For gamers.

Remote worker

Video meetings, cloud files, and work apps. Upload counts. 100 to 300 Mbps usually fits. For work.

Heavy downloader or creator

Big downloads, video uploads, and backups. Faster plans mostly cut waiting time. 500 Mbps to 1 Gig usually fits.

Big household

Five or more people, many screens at once. 300 to 500 Mbps usually fits.

FCC home broadband benchmark for 2024: 100 Mbps down and 20 up. US median home in 2Q26: about 482 GB a month. A common plan band in 3Q25: 200 to 400 Mbps. These ranges are example guidance, not a quote for your home.

Sources: FCC Broadband Speed Guide, FCC Household Broadband Guide, FCC 2024 speed benchmark, Broadband usage report, 2Q26, and Broadband usage report, 3Q25. Figures marked as a usual fit are estimates built from those sources, with room for several activities at once.
